How to read a candle— one minute, then the whole library makes sense
One candle = one slice of time. A 5-minute candle is five minutes of trading squeezed into one shape. The body spans where the period opened and closed; the wicks reach out to the furthest price touched in either direction. Green means it closed above where it opened, red means below — that's a convention, not a law, and some charts use different colours.
The part almost nobody explains

A pattern only exists on the timeframe you're looking at. Four one-hour candles roll into a single four-hour candle, and the shape that appears is often nothing like the four that made it. Watch what these four become:

The maths is simple and worth memorising: the rolled-up candle opens where the first one opened, closes where the last one closed, and its wicks reach the highest high and the lowest low of the group. Everything that happened in between disappears. That is why a setup you can see on the 5-minute may not exist at all on the hourly — and why "what timeframe?" is the first question to ask about any pattern.

Where the numbers come from

Divide any Fibonacci number by the next and the answer converges on 0.618. Skip one and you get 0.382, skip two and you get 0.236, and the square root of 0.618 gives 0.786. The whole ladder falls out of one sequence.

The exception is 50%, which is not a Fibonacci ratio at all. It arrived through Dow theory, survived because the middle of a move is a genuine decision point, and every charting package ships it anyway.

How to place it without fooling yourself

Anchor to one obvious, finished swing: the impulse anybody would point at, not the one that makes your idea work. Wick to wick or body to body is a preference, but pick one and keep it, because switching mid-analysis is how a level appears out of nothing.

And if you catch yourself hunting for the swing that drops a line exactly where you already wanted to buy, you have your answer without the tool.

What the evidence actually says

Batchelor and Ramyar (Cass Business School, 2006) tested retracement and projection ratios in the Dow against what chance alone would produce, found no significant difference, and concluded the idea has no scientific rationale behind it. The Socionomics Institute reviewed the same data and read it the other way.

So hold it honestly. The ladder organises a pullback and marks prices a lot of resting orders share, which is not nothing. It is not an edge by itself.

      Your actual edge here—

      Losses and the gains that undo them are not symmetric. Down 20% needs +25%. Down 50% needs +100%. This curve is the entire argument for small position sizes, and it is pure arithmetic, not opinion.

      The one formula that matters

      position size = risk dollars ÷ distance to stop

      Size from the stop, never from the account balance or from how much you like the idea. If your stop is $2 away and you are risking $200, you buy 100 shares. The instrument changes, the arithmetic does not.

      What changed in 2026

      The pattern day trader rule is gone. The SEC approved FINRA’s amendments to Rule 4210 on 14 April 2026 and they took effect 4 June 2026, removing the $25,000 minimum equity requirement, the four-trades-in-five-days counter, and the PDT designation itself, replaced by risk-based intraday margin (the ordinary $2,000 margin minimum still applies). Older guides still tell you the rule caps your day trades under $25k. It no longer does, which means the discipline that rule used to impose on small accounts is now entirely yours to supply.

      Hedging is buying insurance, and insurance always costs money.

      A hedge that is free is not a hedge, it is a trade with the upside sold off. The right question is never "how do I remove the risk" but "which specific loss can I not tolerate, and what am I willing to pay every year to cap it". Everything below follows from answering that honestly.

      CONTRACT SELECTION

      Eight checks, in order, before the ticket.

      Most bad options trades are not bad reads. They are correct reads expressed through the wrong contract, where the spread, the expiry, or the earnings date took the money that the direction earned.

      Kahneman and Tversky’s value function, drawn with their published 1992 parameters. A loss registers roughly 2.25 times as hard as a gain of the same size. Every discipline problem below is downstream of this one curve: it is why you take the small profit and let the loss run.

      You are not competing with the market. You are competing with the version of yourself that shows up after three losses.

      Almost every rule on the risk desk exists because a normal, healthy human brain produces predictable errors under uncertainty and financial pressure. The errors are not a character flaw and knowing about them does not remove them. What removes them is deciding in advance, in writing, while calm.

      Mark Douglas built his framework on five plainly stated truths, the first being simply that anything can happen. Paraphrasing the rest: you do not need to know what happens next in order to make money, wins and losses arrive in random order inside an edge, an edge is only a probability rather than a promise, and every moment in the market is genuinely new. Accept those and most of the emotional weight of a single trade evaporates.

      TOOL // EXPECTED MOVE

      Expected Move

      The range the options market is pricing, from implied volatility and time.

      ±1 standard deviation (≈68%)
      —
      Chance of finishing beyond ±1σ
      —
      Chance of touching ±1σ
      —

      Touch probability is roughly double the probability of finishing there. This is the number that matters for a stop, a short strike, or anything that triggers on the way rather than at the end — and it is the one most people never look up.

      EM ≈ price × IV × √(days/365). This is a one-standard-deviation band — price finishes outside it roughly a third of the time. It’s a probability cone, not a fence.

      TOOL // FUTURES SPEC SHEET

      Futures Spec Sheet

      The multiplier and tick value behind every point — the numbers you must have before sizing a futures trade.

      Your stop distance in ticks × the tick value = your dollar risk. Micro contracts (MES, MNQ, MCL…) are 1/10 the size and exist so you can learn without full-size risk. Specs are standard references — confirm with your broker.
